## Pack R — larger-sample statistical agreement and production stability (#148)

**Two questions must have separate answers:** (1) do the implemented RORB/Python populations and frequency methods agree to a declared practically meaningful tolerance; (2) is one production run of 1,000 events sufficiently stable for the selected outputs? A small difference between two particular runs answers neither reliably.

### Study population and protocol

1. Extend the existing #148 research harness and retain its completed synthetic studies unchanged. Admit one named physical catchment with authentic source IFD/TP/loss data, supported features, fixed engine/profile, physical sites, durations and probability support. Start with one duration and the required output sites; add the declared critical-duration neighbourhood after the first comparison is informative. Add a second contrasting source-backed catchment only if needed for a broader claim. Synthetic controls remain useful but do not replace this physical case.
2. Freeze a machine-readable protocol before confirmatory results: hypotheses, exact source/profile versions, all outputs to which a joint claim applies, probability support/tails, sample allocations, independent replication strategy, absolute/relative margins, confidence method, cost caps and stopping decisions. Analyse Python/native input laws separately from their final reported peaks.
3. Establish native fresh-stream behaviour under N. Repeated deterministic native replays cannot be counted as independent replications. If independent native streams cannot be supported, retain descriptive/conditional comparisons and identify the limitation; do not manufacture two-sample confidence or require cloning the native RNG as an automatic prerequisite.

### Proposed staged budgets

These are planning settings, conditional on actual native count limits and the timing pilot; they are not a claim of sufficiency or permission to launch every combination automatically.

| Stage | Events per implementation, duration and replication | Replications | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Intake/timing | Smallest valid evidenced native allocation | 1-3 | Validate counts, record completeness, runtime and random-stream observability; no equivalence conclusion |
| Baseline screen | 1,000 (for example 50 x 20) | 30 independent runs in each implementation | Locate output variability, potential bias and useful next budget |
| Larger screen | 5,000 and/or 10,000 (50 x 100/200 where valid) | Prespecified pilot allocation, normally 30 each for the selected budget | Test whether the discrepancy contracts with sampling noise or persists |
| Reference refinement | 50,000-100,000 or an alternative justified by measured precision/native limits | Multiple independent batches, count frozen from pilot cost/variance | Estimate each implementation's limiting curve with remaining uncertainty; not ground truth |
| Confirmation | Selected production budget(s), including 1,000 if evaluating it | Fresh independent runs; 100 is a candidate for a whole-run reliability study | Confirm the predeclared agreement/stability conclusion without reusing the selection pilot |

Report both native and Python costs. For 30 replications at 1,000 events, two implementations require 60,000 routed event evaluations **per duration and scenario**. Physical output sites share those events. A study with additional durations, native controls or reference runs must include them in the frozen total. Before activating any stage, including timing, replace every range, and/or choice and unspecified replication count with an exact allocation and finite event, whole-stage elapsed-time, per-cohort timeout and memory/storage caps. The table above is a planning menu, not an executable blanket budget. Hold event budgets per duration/scenario equal across implementations for the principal efficiency comparison; separately identify extra reference work. If cost exceeds the cap, reduce the declared scope or report cost-limited unresolved. Count attempted, successful, failed and excluded events separately. Repeated printed AEP summary rows are not additional simulations. Study changing stratum count separately from increasing draws per stratum.

### Metrics and statistical decisions

- **Input laws:** compare within-stratum AEP CDFs/quantiles, probability-to-depth residuals at comparable probabilities, IL factor CDF/quantiles/atoms, TP probabilities conditional on AEP/duration, and important joint/conditional relationships. Use actual native strata/weights and account for printed rounding. Pooled unweighted IID tests do not apply automatically to stratified data.
- **Hydrological outputs:** compare weighted survival curves and selected peak quantiles per physical site/duration, the duration envelope, zero-runoff probability and critical-duration selection frequency. Preserve native printed summary comparison and common-policy reaggregation as separate results. A stable envelope magnitude need not imply one uniquely stable critical duration when curves nearly tie; report selection probabilities and a prespecified near-tie rule separately. Full hydrograph diagnostics are selected attribution checks, not a requirement to store every hydrograph.
- **Equivalence:** declare practical margins before confirmation. Report 1%, 2% and 5% relative peak-difference bands as sensitivity cases, plus a catchment-specific absolute floor near zero. For output j use a declared margin such as `Delta_j=max(q_abs_j, r*Q_scale_j)`, with the absolute floor in m3/s and the reference scale frozen from the pilot before confirmation. Do not reuse the earlier synthetic study's 0.002 arbitrary-unit floor as a catchment tolerance. Define separate absolute-probability margins for CDF/AEP differences and zero-runoff probability. Select a primary research margin and document its rationale; these are project study choices, not ARR standards or an automatic engineering acceptance. For example, require an appropriately calibrated simultaneous confidence interval for each selected discrepancy to lie within its prespecified margin. Failure to reject a difference, visually overlapping curves or overlapping separate confidence intervals is not an equivalence test.
- **Both uncertainty sources:** native and Python Monte Carlo error, finite-reference error, printed precision and any residual preparation/profile discrepancy must remain separate. A large native run is not exact truth. Reference batches must be independent of pilot and confirmation batches. Combine their stratum CDF contributions with correct revised weights before inversion; do not average small-batch quantiles and call that the limiting population quantile. Rounding widens uncertainty or prevents a verdict; it must not be hidden by enlarging the practical margin after observing results. Do not assess a data-selected point with an ordinary fixed-point interval without justification.
- **Respect the design:** use independent whole-run replications or a justified within-stratum resampling/variance method; preserve weights and shared site/duration dependence. Quantile atoms/ties and low-density tails need justified inference. Do not apply smooth-quantile asymptotics or an ordinary IID KS/bootstrap test blindly. Use independent exact controls to verify formulas, a method/assumption argument for coverage, and a separately costed synthetic coverage/power study with uncertainty on measured coverage and sensitivity to meaningful differences. A handful of passing test cases is not proof of nominal coverage.
- **Joint confidence:** freeze the allocation of confidence/error across both implementations, all jointly claimed outputs, uncertain reference bands and any whole-run success-rate inference. Two separate 95% statements do not establish one joint 95% claim. Use conservative pass classification when reference uncertainty intersects a tolerance boundary, and report the actual joint confidence justified by the chosen construction.
- **Single-run stability:** report the spread and probability of all required outputs from one production run meeting the declared error band. The standard error of the average of 30 runs is not the uncertainty of a single run. Against the joint reference uncertainty region, classify each run's complete output vector as definite pass, definite failure or indeterminate. Adequacy requires a lower confidence bound on definite-pass probability to meet the declared target. Inadequacy requires an upper confidence bound on possible-pass probability, including indeterminate runs, to fall below it; otherwise unresolved. Retain the existing one-sided binomial method where applicable, with the allocated confidence allowance above, if evaluating a 95% whole-run reliability target. Pilot selection and fresh confirmation remain separate; no repeatedly inspected ordinary CI stopping rule.
- **Scale and attribution:** plot bias/discrepancy and between-run spread against budget. Where IID conditional assumptions hold, assess the expected inverse-square-root noise trend, allowing quantile nonregularity. A persistent difference triggers the predeclared C attribution sequence: probability convention/support, strata/draw law, IFD, IL/TP/dependence, effective preparation, aggregation/tails/inverse, printed rounding, and only a concrete residual routing-profile mismatch. More runs must not be used to conceal a systematic difference.

**Acceptance:** a reproducible report with protocol, permitted compact evidence, scientific figures, per-output uncertainty/margins, event/runtime cost, explicit 1,000-run adequate/inadequate/unresolved verdicts, and a cross-implementation disposition of `equivalent_within_declared_margins`, `difference_explained`, `material_difference_unresolved` or `insufficient_evidence`. An explanation must be supported by a controlled comparison or identified rule effect, not a plausible narrative. Explained differences do not automatically count as native compatibility; document whether they require a fix or an explicitly accepted alternative profile. N independently reviews the conclusions. Keep parent #9 and draft #151 open for any remaining required claim.

## Sources and interpretation

- [ARR Book 4 v4.2, chapter 4](https://www.arr-software.org/pdfs/ARR_190514_Book4_V4.2.pdf): stratification, probability weighting and joint-input modelling. Published guidance is not a complete specification of the selected executable.
- [Nathan and Weinmann (2013)](https://arr.ga.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0017/40562/ARR_DiscussionPaper_MonteCarlo.pdf): historical Monte Carlo probability treatment and examples.
- [HARC initial-loss correction](https://harc.com.au/forums/topic/rorbwin-version-6-32/) and [TP selection](https://harc.com.au/forums/topic/selection-of-temporal-patterns/): dated implementation evidence to reconcile with the current native profile.
- [Dong and Nakayama (2020)](https://web.njit.edu/~marvin/papers/qtut-r2.pdf): quantile estimation and confidence procedures depend on the simulation design; adapt and verify assumptions for this stratified/discrete problem.
- [Lakens (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177): prespecified equivalence bounds and why a nonsignificant difference is insufficient. This supplies the general inferential principle, not a hydrological tolerance or an off-the-shelf test for weighted flood quantiles.
- [Latest #150 disposition](https://github.com/Chain-Frost/python-runoff-routing/issues/150#issuecomment-6923794218): native F1-F6 remain open; the falsified native-style 20% star was removed. Existing `!` is a separate conditional project diagnostic.
